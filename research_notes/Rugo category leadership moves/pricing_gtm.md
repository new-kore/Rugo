# Pricing & Go-to-Market Models for Trucking TMS / Fleet Software (2023–2026)

Method note: 17 search/fetch calls (Nov 2026). Several third-party pricing-roundup sites (hemut.com, truxello.com, tomtunguz.com) could not be fetched in this sandbox (network blocked), so some figures rely on search-result snippets of those pages. Most vendors do NOT publish list prices; many numbers below are third-party estimates and conflict. All currency is USD unless marked CAD.

## 1. Published or reported prices by vendor

### Takeaway
Telematics/ELD clusters at ~$20–35 USD per vehicle/month (hardware ~$100–200), with Samsara on 3-year contracts and Motive on ~12-month terms. Carrier TMS pricing spans three bands: SMB self-serve ($25–$150/mo flat or per user/truck), mid-market quote-based (~$300–$500+/mo floors), and enterprise platform ($2,000+/mo, e.g., Rose Rocket). Load boards cost ~$150–$170/user/month at the "Pro" tier.

### Inferences
- A Canadian SMB carrier stacks: ELD (~$35–45 CAD/truck) + load board (~$200+ CAD/user) + TMS. A TMS priced below the load board per seat, or bundled with ELD/load-board integration, is easy to justify.
- The "missing middle" price band is clear: $25–$150/mo tools (TruckingOffice, Ascend) vs $290–$2,080/mo (Truckbase, Alvys, Rose Rocket). Per-truck pricing in the ~$30–$80 CAD range would sit between them.
- Samsara's 3-year lock-in and ETFs, and DAT's reported price increases, give a challenger something to market against (monthly or annual, no hardware lock-in).

### Gaps
- No reliable published prices found for Tai TMS, Ditat, Geotab (incl. CAD reseller pricing), or Samsara/Motive Canadian list prices.
- Setup/implementation fees mostly unpublished except Rose Rocket's included 90-day implementation.

## 2. Which pricing models win by fleet size

### Takeaway
Small fleets (1–20) buy cheap, transparent, monthly self-serve tiers (often banded by truck count) or free/fintech-subsidized software. Mid-market (20–200) buys quote-based per-truck or per-load plans with onboarding. Enterprise buys platform contracts ($100K+ ACV), multi-product, multi-year (Samsara model).

### Cited Findings
- TruckingOffice uses truck-count bands ($25 → $90 Basic) aimed at owner-operators/small fleets — [Toro TMS](https://www.torotms.com/blog/best-software-for-small-trucking-company)
- Motive ~$5K average contract value (SMB/mid-market heavy) with 126% enterprise NDR — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/motive-s-1/); ~100,000–120,000 customers per summaries — [IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/ipo/motive-technologies-ex-keeptruckin/)
- Samsara's growth driven by $100K+ ARR customers (61% of ARR), multi-product — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/5-interesting-learnings-from-samsara-at-1-9-billion-in-arr/)
- Alvys prices on load volume; Truckbase offers per-truck/per-driver/per-load/flat — [CarrierOS](https://carrierosai.com/carrieros-ai-tms-comparison/), [Yolda](https://yolda.ai/blog/best-transportation-management-software)
- Rose Rocket's Series B (June 2023, $38M USD) was explicitly to expand into larger fleets / enterprise — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230620643042/en/)
- AtoB CEO: "90% of our potential customers have five trucks" — software/fintech targeting tiny fleets — [AtoB funding coverage](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240919045000/en/AtoB-Announces-New-Funding-and-Growth-Milestones)
- Denim QuickPay is free to brokers and carriers; monetized via factoring fees — [Denim pricing](https://www.denim.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- Freemium only works when there is a second revenue stream (fuel card, factoring, payments, load board). Ascend's reported free-tier removal after acquisition suggests pure freemium TMS was hard to sustain without that.
- Per-truck pricing tracks how carriers think about cost (per truck per month, like ELD) and grows with them; per-load pricing suits brokers and carriers with volatile volume.

### Gaps
- No public cohort data comparing freemium vs paid conversion in trucking TMS.

## 3. Fintech-embedded monetization

### Takeaway
Payments are where the margin is: fuel cards earn interchange (open-loop) or merchant fees (closed-loop) and win customers with ~$0.25–0.50/gal discounts. Factoring earns ~1.5–5% of invoice value. QuickPay lets brokers charge carriers ~1–5%. Several fintechs give away software and make money on money movement.

### Cited Findings
- Motive Spend Management (fuel/fleet card) grows with payment volume; it is a small line in the S-1 — [SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646681/000162828025058773/motive-sx1.htm); card earns interchange and uses in-cab ELD data for underwriting — [App Economy Insights](https://www.appeconomyinsights.com/p/how-motive-makes-money)
- AtoB: open-loop Mastercard/Visa rails, earns interchange per swipe (vs WEX/Corpay closed-loop fees) — [Teardown](https://www.teardown.ai/companies/atob); 2022: zero-fee card, 5¢/gal discount, revenue expected >$20M (older) — [Forbes 2022](https://www.forbes.com/sites/amyfeldman/2022/08/16/with-fuel-costs-high-trucker-focused-fintech-atob-reaches-800-million-valuation/); Sep 2024: $130M Series C (General Catalyst, Mastercard participating), 500% revenue/volume growth claimed, Uber Freight co-branded card — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240919045000/en/AtoB-Announces-New-Funding-and-Growth-Milestones); 25,000–30,000+ fleets reported (sources conflict) — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/atob-transportation-focused-payment-solutions-company-raises-130-million/)
- Relay: geofenced single-use payment codes for fuel and lumper fees rather than cards — [Embedded Finance Medium](https://medium.com/@sambokher/embedded-finance-in-trucking-4c4cf74808e2)
- Outgo: factoring plus a debit card whose interchange subsidizes the service; cashback effectively lowers the factoring rate; no pump discounts — [TruckingWay](https://www.truckingway.com/outgo-factoring-review/), [Unit case study](https://www.unit.co/case-study/outgo)
- Denim: QuickPay free, brokers keep 100% of their carrier QuickPay fee (typically 1–5%); separate Express Factoring fee; >$2B payments processed by Nov 2024 — [Denim QuickPay](https://www.denim.com/blog/quickpay-denim-vs-the-alternatives), [Denim news](https://www.denim.com/news/denim-celebrates-2-billion-in-payments-processed); acquired by Truckstop — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2941996); Truckstop Financial processed $3B in first year — [AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/carriers-got-paid-faster-and-brokers-earned-more-as-truckstop-financial-processed-3-billion-in-its-first-year)
- RTS fuel card averages 25¢/gal discount (company-stated) — [RTS](https://www.rtsinc.com/lp/trucking-factoring-services); factoring reported 1.5–3.5% per 30 days or 2–5%, advances up to 95–97% (third-party, conflicting) — [comparison](https://usatruckerchoice.pages.dev/head-to-head/rts-vs-triumph-factoring/)
- Triumph claims 51¢/gal average fuel savings (Q1 2026 in-network, self-reported) — [Triumph](https://triumph.io/make-cents/); factoring ~1.5–5%, 97% advance (third-party) — [comparison](https://usatruckerchoice.pages.dev/head-to-head/rts-vs-triumph-factoring/)

### Inferences
- Rough math (inference, not sourced): a truck burning ~20,000 gal/yr at ~$4/gal spends ~$80K/yr on fuel; ~1–2% interchange would be ~$800–$1,600/truck/yr, comparable to or above a $50–100/truck/month SaaS fee. Factoring at 2–3% of ~$200K/yr truck revenue is ~$4K–6K/truck/yr. That is why fintech-first players can give software away.
- In Canada, a TMS partnering with a Canadian factor or a fuel-card network (e.g., via a BaaS provider) could subsidize SMB tiers. Canadian interchange and fuel-network specifics were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No public take-rate or revenue for Relay, Outgo, Denim; Motive's spend-management revenue not broken out.
- Canadian fuel-card / factoring players and rates not covered.

## 4. Distribution channels that worked

### Takeaway
The best-documented channels are insurance telematics discounts (Progressive Smart Haul + preferred ELD vendors), embedded marketplace/payments partnerships (AtoB + Uber Freight; Denim + Tai; Truckstop acquiring Denim), and load-board ownership (DAT/Truckstop bundling TMS and payments). Little hard data was found on creator/YouTube or association channels.

### Cited Findings
- Progressive Smart Haul: preferred ELDs are Geotab, Motive, Omnitracs; new enrollees get at least 5% off, up to 15%+ with a clean record; non-preferred ELDs get 3% — [Progressive](https://www.progressivecommercial.com/commercial-auto-insurance/truck-insurance/smart-haul/); 2022 Motive launch cited 5–12% — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/motive-partners-with-progressive/)
- Some applicants may be required to enroll and switch to Motive devices to get coverage (an insurer pushing ELD adoption) — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/progressives-mandatory-eld-switch-some-small-trucking-fleets-may-be-required-to-switch-eld-providers); Progressive later ended new Motive dashcam enrollments — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=575239)
- AtoB distribution via Uber Freight co-branded card and Maverik/Kum & Go discount network (840+ locations) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240919045000/en/AtoB-Announces-New-Funding-and-Growth-Milestones)
- Denim integrated with Tai TMS for broker payments (TMS-as-channel for fintech) — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2099869)
- Load boards bundle TMS: DAT Broker TMS from ~$100/mo, Truckstop ITS Dispatch ~$50/mo — [FreightVero](https://www.freightvero.com/guides/tms-pricing-freight-brokers/)

### Inferences
- For Canada: insurer partnerships (telematics data for premium discounts) and integrations with ELDs already present in Canadian fleets (Isaac, Geotab resellers, Motive) are likely the most defensible channels. Association channels (OTA/CTA) are plausible but unverified.

### Gaps
- No sourced evidence found on YouTube/TikTok trucking creator ROI, dealer channels, or OTA/CTA vendor partnerships with TMS players. No Rose Rocket–OTA partnership found.

## 5. Switching costs, onboarding, churn

### Takeaway
Switching is mostly a data-migration and integration project (rates, customers, EDI/carrier connections). Enterprise implementations run ~90 days (Rose Rocket includes a 90-day white-glove implementation). Contract lock-ins (Samsara 3-yr, DAT auto-renew) raise switching costs. No quantitative churn surveys were found.

### Cited Findings
- Rose Rocket includes a 90-day white-glove implementation — [TenTrucks](https://tentrucks.com/blog/rose-rocket-tms-pricing-alternatives)
- A TMS switch is "as much a data project as a software project"; rate-data migration ~1–2 weeks; risks are carrier communication, rate migration, and exception-workflow handoff — [Nuvocargo 2026](https://www.nuvocargo.com/blog-posts/how-to-replace-tms)
- EDI/partner connectivity is labor-intensive during implementation (older, 2014) — [Talking Logistics](https://talkinglogistics.com/2014/09/15/companies-replacing-tms/)
- Few LSPs have a long-term plan to upgrade or replace their TMS (inertia) — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/status-quo-at-what-cost)
- Samsara early-termination liability example ~$18,150 for 25 trucks — [ai-eld.com](https://ai-eld.com/insights/eld-comparison-samsara-motive-geotab-verizon)
- 2026 article on why TMS implementations break down — [Logistics Viewpoints](https://logisticsviewpoints.com/2026/09/27/why-tms-implementations-break-downand-what-high-performance-deployments-get-right/)

### Inferences
- A wedge for a challenger: free assisted migration (CSV/QuickBooks/legacy TMS import), go-live within days for small fleets, no long contract. Once installed, accounting, IFTA, settlements, and factoring integrations create the stickiness.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2026 quantitative data on TMS churn rates or ranked switching reasons for carriers.

## 6. Land-and-expand and network effects

### Takeaway
Telematics leaders land with ELD/compliance and expand into safety (dash cams), then spend/fuel cards (Motive), driving 115–126% NRR. Rose Rocket positions as a "network-centric" TMS connecting shippers, carriers, and partners. Load boards (Truckstop) expand into payments by acquisition (Denim).

### Cited Findings
- Samsara: 96% of $100K+ customers use 2+ products, 69% use 3+; ~115% NRR — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/5-interesting-learnings-from-samsara-at-1-9-billion-in-arr/)
- Motive: subscription growth of $57.1M in 9 months, $18.8M from new customers (i.e., most from existing-customer expansion); spend management layered on telematics — [SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1646681/000162828025058773/motive-sx1.htm)
- Rose Rocket: network grew 47x since prior round (2023); 9x customer growth in the 12 months before Series A (2021, older) — [BusinessWire 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230620643042/en/), [TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/canadian-startup-rose-rocket-raises-series-a-to-scale-its-transport-software-business); acquired Toronto-based Centro (Sept 2025) and positions TMS.ai as an "AI-native TMS" — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/rose-rocket)
- Truckstop acquired Denim to add factoring/payments to its load board network — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2941996)

### Inferences
- The proven sequence: land with a must-have, mandate-driven or cash-flow product (ELD, payments, factoring), then expand to TMS, safety, and fuel. For a Canadian TMS: land with small carriers (dispatch + invoicing + factoring/QuickPay), add shipper/broker portals so each customer invites partners. That turns customers into a distribution channel.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on shipper-first vs broker-first land strategies' relative success; no driver-app-first case with numbers.
